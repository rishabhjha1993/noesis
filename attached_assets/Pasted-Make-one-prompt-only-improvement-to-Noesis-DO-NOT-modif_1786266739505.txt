The walkthrough currently explains HOW to read a visual well, but some insights are too generic.

Add the following teaching principle to the existing Noesis analysis prompt:

INSIGHT QUALITY

For every selected region, do not stop at describing what is visible.

After identifying how the viewer should read the region, determine:

"What is the most informative, surprising, contrasting, decision-relevant, or non-obvious insight that is directly supported by this region?"

Prefer insights involving, when clearly visible:

- sharp contrasts
- divergence over time
- dominant contributors
- ratios or magnitude differences
- thresholds
- bottlenecks
- anomalies
- nonlinear changes
- reversals
- trade-offs
- unusually large or small values
- long-term consequences
- relationships that are easy to miss when first viewing the image

Use clearly legible evidence from the visual whenever possible.

For example:

WEAK:
"Higher warming produces larger climate impacts."

STRONGER:
"Drought frequency rises from roughly 2× at +1.5°C to about 4.1× at +4°C, while snow-cover loss increases from about 5% to 26%."

WEAK:
"Sea level increases with warming."

STRONGER:
"The visual shows that sea-level commitment persists for millennia: under +4°C warming, the indicated rise reaches roughly 16 m over 2,000 years and 33 m over 10,000 years."

WEAK:
"Transportation uses a lot of petroleum."

STRONGER:
"Transportation receives about 24.8 of its 28 quads from petroleum, making it one of the strongest single-fuel dependencies in the system."
